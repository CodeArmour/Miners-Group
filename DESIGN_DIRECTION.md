# Miners Group — Website Design Direction

## 1. Design Goal

The Miners Group website should feel like a modern technology ecosystem where people learn, build, collaborate, and move toward real professional opportunities.

It must NOT look like:
- a generic software agency template,
- a university website,
- a course marketplace,
- a bootcamp landing page,
- or a gaming/cyberpunk community.

The design should communicate:

**Ambition + Collaboration + Engineering + Growth + Opportunity**

The visual experience should make students feel:
> "I want to become part of this."

Companies should feel:
> "This is an organized technical group I can trust and collaborate with."

---

## 2. Core Visual Concept

### Concept Name
**The Mining Journey**

The design language is inspired by the idea of discovering potential, refining skills, building valuable things, and progressing deeper into real engineering experience.

Do not use literal mining illustrations, pickaxes, helmets, caves, or cryptocurrency imagery.

Instead, express the concept abstractly through:
- connected nodes,
- paths,
- layers,
- grids,
- progressive levels,
- modular cards,
- subtle depth,
- moving particles,
- and visual transitions between learning and professional work.

The website should visually reinforce the core journey:

**Learn → Practice → Build → Get Mentored → Gain Experience → Work**

---

## 3. Brand Personality

Miners Group should feel:
- Modern
- Technical
- International
- Community-driven
- Serious but approachable
- Youthful without looking childish
- Premium without looking corporate
- Experimental without feeling chaotic
- Professional enough for companies
- Welcoming enough for students

Avoid corporate stock photography.

Prefer:
- real future community photography,
- abstract technical visuals,
- project screenshots,
- code/product interfaces,
- mentor/member portraits,
- workshop imagery,
- diagrams and interface-driven storytelling.

Until real photography exists, use abstract visuals and product/UI compositions rather than fake people.

---

## 4. Color System

### Primary — Electric Indigo
`#5B3DF5`

Use for:
- primary CTAs,
- active states,
- key highlights,
- journey nodes,
- links,
- selected cards,
- subtle glow accents.

### Secondary — Soft Lilac
`#E8DEFF`

Use for:
- soft sections,
- card backgrounds,
- accent surfaces,
- selected tags,
- light visual contrast.

### Deep Ink
`#11111A`

Primary dark text and dark-mode surface.

### Off White
`#F8F8FB`

Main light background.

### Muted Gray
`#72727E`

Secondary body copy.

### Accent Lime
`#CFFF74`

Use sparingly for:
- status dots,
- opportunity labels,
- success/growth indicators,
- hover accents,
- internship/open-program badges.

Indigo remains the dominant brand color.

---

## 5. Theme Direction

Design the first version primarily in **light mode**.

The website should feel bright, spacious, confident, and editorial.

Use dark sections selectively for:
- the Labs/project showcase,
- the company/project CTA,
- important transitions,
- footer.

Do not make the whole website dark.

---

## 6. Typography

Recommended font families:
- Geist
- Inter
- Manrope

Use:
- 64–88px responsive display type for major hero statements,
- bold but controlled H1/H2 typography,
- 17–19px readable body copy,
- 12–14px labels with tracking.

Avoid enormous typography in every section.

---

## 7. Layout System

Use:
- max width around 1240–1320px,
- a 12-column grid,
- generous whitespace,
- asymmetric editorial layouts,
- modular bento compositions,
- strong visual rhythm,
- rounded but not overly bubbly cards.

Avoid repeating the same:
**heading → paragraph → three equal cards**
pattern across the site.

Each major section should have its own composition.

---

## 8. Navigation

Create a floating sticky navbar.

Left:
**Miners Group** wordmark/logo.

Center:
- About
- What We Do
- Programs
- Projects
- Community

Right:
- `For Companies`
- `Join Miners` primary CTA

Style:
- translucent light surface,
- backdrop blur,
- thin border,
- compact height,
- soft rounded container.

On scroll:
- slightly reduce navbar height,
- increase surface opacity,
- preserve smooth transitions.

---

## 9. Hero

Use a two-column asymmetric hero.

### Left
- eyebrow: `MINERS GROUP`
- headline from the content brief
- short supporting copy
- primary CTA: `Join Miners Group`
- secondary CTA: `Explore the Ecosystem`

### Right
Build an interactive **Miners Ecosystem Map** connecting:
- Academy
- Labs
- Mentorship
- Software
- Talent — future

Represent them as floating nodes/cards connected by thin animated paths.

Below the hero copy, display:

**Learn → Practice → Build → Get Mentored → Gain Experience → Work**

Animate the stages subtly in sequence.

Do not use generic 3D blobs or stock illustrations.

---

## 10. The Problem / Why Miners Exists

Do not jump immediately into service cards.

Create an editorial storytelling section about the gap between learning programming and being ready for real software work.

Suggested composition:
- large statement on the left,
- vertical real student questions on the right.

Examples:
- What should I learn next?
- How do professional teams actually work?
- How do I get experience before my first job?
- What should I put in my portfolio?
- How do I know if I am job-ready?

Visually move from **Knowledge** toward **Experience** while scrolling.

End with:

**Miners Group exists to close this gap.**

---

## 11. The Miners Journey

This should be a signature interaction.

Do not use six ordinary cards.

Desktop:
create a horizontal connected path.

Stages:

01 — Learn  
02 — Practice  
03 — Build  
04 — Get Mentored  
05 — Gain Experience  
06 — Work

Each stage includes:
- number,
- title,
- short explanation,
- simple icon.

The connecting line fills as the section scrolls.

Mobile:
convert it into a vertical timeline.

---

## 12. Miners Ecosystem / Pillars

Heading:

**One ecosystem. Multiple ways to grow.**

Use a bento layout.

### Academy
Large card.
Visual: learning tracks, workshops, skill paths.

### Labs
Large dark card.
Visual: project board, repositories, prototype UI.

### Mentorship
Tall card.
Visual: mentor/member relationship or conversation flow.

### Software
Wide professional card.
Visual: delivery pipeline or product interfaces.

### Talent
Smaller `Coming Later` card.
Visual: opportunity network.

Avoid four equal generic cards.

---

## 13. Dual Audience Split

This is essential.

Create one large split section.

### For Students & Developers

Headline:

**Turn what you know into what you can prove.**

Focus on:
- training,
- mentorship,
- projects,
- internships,
- guidance.

CTA:

**Start Your Journey**

### For Companies

Headline:

**Build with an engineering community.**

Focus on:
- software implementation,
- web/mobile,
- AI,
- cloud/DevOps,
- technical collaboration.

CTA:

**Start a Project**

Give each half distinct visual treatment while keeping one brand system.

---

## 14. Projects / Miners Labs

Create a premium project gallery.

Do not invent case studies.

Until real projects exist, use:
- internal projects,
- experiments,
- clearly labeled placeholders,
- `Coming Soon` states.

Project cards can contain:
- project name,
- purpose,
- category,
- technologies,
- status,
- team format,
- case study link when available.

Use large visual cards rather than small portfolio tiles.

---

## 15. Community

Headline:

**You should not have to grow alone.**

Create a human, layered collage/grid representing:
- coding sessions,
- workshops,
- mentor conversations,
- challenges,
- events,
- team collaboration.

Use small floating labels such as:
- Build Together
- Ask
- Review
- Share
- Mentor
- Learn

CTA:

**Join the Community**

Do not display fake member counts.

---

## 16. Mentorship

Create a visual connection:

**Learner ↔ Mentor**

Surface example questions:
- Which path fits me?
- What should I learn next?
- How can I improve this project?
- Is my portfolio ready?
- How do I prepare for internships?

The section should feel like guidance, not paid coaching.

CTA:

**Explore Mentorship**

- Mobile applications

- AI-powered solutions

- Cloud / DevOps
- Maintenance & support

### Enable
- Technical training
- Consulting
- Workshops

CTA:

**Work With Miners**

## 18. Opportunities / Internships

Possible future types:
- Internship
- Training Program

- Mentor
- Community Volunteer

Do not invent active openings.

If none exist:

**No active opportunities right now — join the community to hear about future programs.**

CTA:

**Explore Opportunities**

---

## 19. For Companies CTA

Use a dark, high-contrast section.

Headline:

**Have an idea worth building?**

Supporting copy:
Work with Miners Group on web, mobile, custom software, AI, cloud, training, or technical collaboration.

Actions:
- **Start a Project**
- **Book a Coffee Chat**

Show a compact process:

**Idea → Scope → Team → Build → Review → Deliver**

---

## 20. Final Community CTA

Bring the page back to the mission.

Headline:

**Your next step should not depend on figuring everything out alone.**

Supporting copy:
Join Miners Group to learn with direction, build with others, receive guidance, and turn technical knowledge into meaningful experience.

Primary CTA:
**Join Miners Group**

Secondary:
**Explore Programs**

## 21. Footer

Dark footer with generous spacing.

Columns:
- Explore
- Programs
- Companies
- Community
- Contact

Brand copy:

**Miners Group**  
From learning to real software experience.

Only show social links when real URLs are provided.

---

## 22. Motion & Interaction

Use motion to tell the story.

Good:
- gentle fade/slide entrance,
- journey progress,
- animated node connections,
- staggered cards,
- subtle hover lift,
- restrained magnetic CTAs,
- smooth accordions,
- light parallax,
- cursor proximity on ecosystem nodes.

Avoid:
- constant glow,
- bouncing elements,
- excessive floating objects,
- scroll hijacking,
- long intro loaders,
- unnecessary WebGL.

Honor `prefers-reduced-motion`.

---

## 23. Background Treatment

Main background:
off-white.

Use subtle:
- dot grids,
- thin technical lines,
- low-opacity radial indigo/lilac gradients,
- restrained network patterns.

Never sacrifice readability.

---

## 24. Card Style

Use:
- 18–28px radius,
- thin borders,
- soft shadows,
- generous padding,
- controlled depth.

Do not use glassmorphism everywhere.

Reserve glass mainly for:
- navbar,
- ecosystem nodes,
- overlays,
- selected interactive surfaces.

---

## 25. Responsive Behavior

### Desktop
Full editorial/asymmetric compositions.

### Tablet
Reduce offsets and simplify node connections.

### Mobile
- hero becomes single column,
- ecosystem becomes stacked or scrollable,
- journey becomes vertical,
- bento cards stack logically,
- audience split becomes sequential panels,
- navbar becomes compact menu.

Do not simply scale desktop down.

---

## 26. Accessibility

Required:
- good contrast,
- visible keyboard focus,
- semantic headings,
- labels on forms,
- usable tap targets,
- reduced motion support,
- meaningful alt text,
- no important text embedded inside images.

---

## 27. Content Safety / Credibility Rules

Use `WEBSITE_CONTENT.md` as the messaging source of truth.

Never invent:
- customers,
- testimonials,
- statistics,
- member counts,
- universities,
- partners,
- projects,
- awards,
- mentor identities,
- office locations,
- outcomes.

Where data does not exist, use an honest empty state or `Coming Soon`.

## 30. Success Criteria

A visitor should understand within seconds that:

1. Miners Group helps developers move from learning to practical experience.
2. It includes training, projects, mentorship, internships, guidance, and community.
3. It also works with companies on software and technical collaboration.
4. Students and companies have separate, obvious journeys.
5. The brand feels modern, credible, international, and distinctive.
6. The site does not make claims that Miners Group cannot yet prove.

The intended final impression is:

**Miners is a place where potential becomes experience.**
